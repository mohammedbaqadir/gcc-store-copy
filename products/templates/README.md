# Product Templates

## Purpose

Product templates define the product-specific information that helps the skill write useful, fact-grounded customer copy.

They are **guidance for organizing and interpreting product facts**, not a universal database schema. The canonical product record remains the source of truth.

A template should help the agent answer three questions:

1. **What facts matter for this product type?**
2. **Which customer decisions can those facts support?**
3. **What must never be inferred when those facts are missing?**

## How to use a template

1. Identify the broad product type.
2. Read that template before drafting.
3. Find the supplied product facts that map to the template.
4. Prefer high-value, verified facts over completeness for its own sake.
5. Do not invent a missing fact merely because the template lists it as useful.
6. Surface important gaps when they materially limit accurate or useful copy.

The templates are deliberately broad. Individual products may need additional facts that are not listed here. The "Prioritize first" section in each template is a ranking aid, not a required-field schema. Missing a listed fact never licenses invention.

## Shared fact groups

Across product types, useful facts usually fall into these groups:

- **Identity:** what the thing/service is and its exact variant.
- **Core offer:** what the customer actually gets.
- **Characteristics:** important attributes, specifications, contents, or scope.
- **Customer use:** how, when, where, or by whom it is used.
- **Customer benefit:** what a verified characteristic helps the customer do or understand.
- **Constraints:** eligibility, compatibility, limitations, exclusions, or prerequisites.
- **Logistics:** delivery, access, fulfillment, timing, packaging, or booking details.
- **Commercial terms:** quantity, duration, validity, revisions, included items, or other purchase-relevant terms.
- **Proof/claims:** certifications, guarantees, test results, origin, or other claims only when explicitly supported.

Do not assume every group applies to every product.
