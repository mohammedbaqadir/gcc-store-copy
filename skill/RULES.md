# Rules

## Rule Priority and Scope

This file defines the concrete writing rules used to transform supported product information into customer-facing product copy.

These rules operationalize the target defined in `STYLE.md`.

They do not replace the style guide, vocabulary, forbidden-expression list, QA process, product templates, or workflow.

When requirements interact:

1. explicit task requirements control the requested output format and objective;
2. task instructions never authorize invented or unsupported product facts;
3. product-specific source information and requirements control relevant product details;
4. these rules govern how supported information is written;
5. `STYLE.md` defines the desired overall character;
6. preferences are subordinate to the requirements above.

Do not create a special rule merely to solve one unusual product. Prefer rules that remain useful across product categories.

---

## 1. Source Facts

### Use Supported Facts

Every factual statement about a product must be supported by available product information.

This includes claims about:

- characteristics;
- specifications;
- materials;
- ingredients;
- dimensions;
- compatibility;
- included items;
- intended uses;
- delivery or fulfillment information;
- performance;
- outcomes;
- limitations;
- any other product-specific fact.

Do not fill gaps using assumptions about what is normally true for similar products.

### Preserve Meaning

Do not change the meaning of source information while making it more attractive.

Do not:

- upgrade a specification;
- broaden a compatibility statement;
- turn a limited feature into a universal claim;
- turn a possibility into a guarantee;
- remove a meaningful limitation.

Natural wording may simplify a fact, but it must remain faithful to the supplied information.

### Missing Information

When information is missing, do not invent it.

If it is not necessary to describe the product, omit it.

If it is necessary, use neutral supported wording or surface the missing information.

### Conflicting Information

When sources conflict, do not silently choose the version that produces better copy.

Use an explicitly established authoritative source when one exists.

Otherwise, avoid relying on the disputed information and surface the conflict when necessary.

---

## 2. Customer Relevance

### Prioritize What Matters

Do not follow the order of the source data mechanically.

Prioritize information according to its usefulness to the customer's decision.

Important identity, use, distinguishing characteristics, and relevant practical information generally deserve more attention than low-value technical details.

### Explain Practical Value

When the relationship is directly supported by the product information, connect an important feature to its practical meaning.

For example:

> "يجي بسعة 256 جيجابايت، فتقدر تحفظ عدد أكبر من التطبيقات والصور والملفات."

The explanation is valid because the stated storage capacity directly relates to the amount of data that can be stored.

Do not create a marketing benefit merely because it sounds like a plausible consequence of a feature.

A feature may be connected to a benefit only when that relationship is directly supported by the supplied information or follows objectively without introducing a new product claim.

### Remove Irrelevant Information

Do not include every available fact simply because it exists.

A fact may be true and still be unsuitable for the main description.

Prioritize information that helps the customer understand, compare, imagine, or choose the product.

---

## 3. Features and Benefits

### State Important Features

Describe important features accurately.

### Connect Features to Customer Use

Where supported, explain what an important feature means in practical use.

Do not make the customer perform unnecessary interpretation of a meaningful specification.

### Never Upgrade the Benefit

Do not transform:

> "مقاوم للماء"

into:

> "يحمي جهازك مهما كانت الظروف."

Do not transform:

> "يحتوي على مكوّن معين"

into:

> "يضمن نتيجة معينة."

Do not turn technical information into stronger promises than the source supports.

When the benefit is uncertain, state the feature itself.

---

## 4. Description Structure

### Structure for Information, Not Formula

There is no mandatory paragraph formula for every product.

Choose a structure that communicates the product naturally and efficiently.

A useful description often moves from:

> identity → important characteristics → practical relevance → supporting details

but the exact structure should change when the product requires it.

### Opening

Start with the product itself and the most useful supported reason the customer may care about it.

Avoid generic openings that could describe almost anything.

Weak:

> "اكتشف تجربة استثنائية تجمع بين الجودة والأناقة والابتكار."

Stronger:

> "شنطة ظهر عملية للاستخدام اليومي، بتقسيمات تساعدك ترتب أغراضك بسهولة."

### Supporting Information

Include the details that meaningfully support understanding or choice.

Do not turn the description into a dump of every available field.

### Closing

A closing is optional.

Use one only when it adds useful context, reinforces a supported value, or clarifies suitability.

Do not add a generic promotional ending simply because the description "needs a conclusion."

---

## 5. Sentence and Paragraph Construction

### Directness

Deliver the useful information without unnecessary introduction.

### Sentence Length

Prefer readable sentence lengths.

Longer sentences are acceptable when related information naturally belongs together.

Do not combine unrelated facts only to reduce paragraph count.

### Information Density

Each sentence should contribute at least one of:

- useful product information;
- meaningful explanation;
- customer-relevant context;
- justified persuasion.

Do not add sentences whose main function is to make the copy sound more impressive.

### Repetition

Do not repeat the same idea in different language merely to increase length.

When a point is already clear, move to new information.

### Do Not Manufacture a Formula

Do not automatically produce:

> generic opening + generic praise + three benefits + "مثالي لـ..." + generic closing.

Previous successful descriptions are evidence of quality, not a rhetorical template that every new product must imitate.

## 6. Saudi Arabic Execution

### Modern Written Arabic

Write in modern Saudi-facing commercial Arabic according to `STYLE.md`.

Do not force dialect into every sentence.

### Natural Familiarity

Use familiar customer-facing phrasing where it improves naturalness or clarity.

Do not insert colloquial expressions simply to appear local.

### Arabic and English Terminology

Use familiar technical or commercial terminology when it is genuinely natural for Saudi customers.

Do not replace clear Arabic with English terminology merely to sound modern.

Do not use literal Arabic translations of industry terms when the familiar term is clearer.

The specific preferred and discouraged terminology belongs in `VOCABULARY.md`.

### Translation

When source information is written in another language, translate the meaning rather than the sentence structure.

The final copy must read like Arabic written for the customer, not translated Arabic.

---

## 7. Claims and Promotional Language

### Supported Claims

Promotional statements are still claims.

A phrase does not become factually safe merely because it is common in advertising.

### Match Claim Strength to Evidence

The strength of the wording must not exceed the strength of the supporting information.

For example, supported suitability should not automatically become a universal guarantee.

### Superlatives

Do not make unsupported superlative claims.

Do not turn every product into:

- الأفضل;
- الأقوى;
- الأجود;
- الأحدث;
- رقم 1;
- لا مثيل له.

Specific prohibited expressions belong in `FORBIDDEN.md`; detection belongs in `QA.md`.

### Customer Promises

Do not promise outcomes that depend on information or circumstances outside the supported product facts.

Be especially careful with claims involving:

- health outcomes;
- performance;
- durability;
- compatibility;
- guaranteed results;
- universal suitability.

Do not strengthen such claims unless the source information explicitly supports them and the task permits them.

---

## 8. Specifications and Exact Information

### Numbers and Units

Preserve supplied numerical information accurately.

Do not round, reinterpret, or convert measurements unless explicitly required.

### Product Names

Preserve official brand names, product names, and model numbers unless the task explicitly requires adaptation.

Do not invent localized names.

### Technical Terms

Preserve technical distinctions that materially affect customer understanding.

Simplify explanations when useful, but do not simplify away an important distinction.

### Variants

Keep variants distinct.

Do not combine different colors, sizes, capacities, models, configurations, or service levels into one statement unless the available data explicitly supports the combination.

---

## 9. Category Adaptation

### Do Not Reuse a Universal Template

Different products answer different customer questions.

Do not mechanically reuse the same structure, number of benefits, or rhetorical pattern across categories.

### Follow the Product Template

The applicable product template defines what kinds of product information may be available and relevant.

It is the authority for product-data structure.

The examples below illustrate possible priorities; they are not mandatory schemas.

Physical products may emphasize use, dimensions, materials, or compatibility.

Food may emphasize product contents, serving context, or relevant handling information when supplied.

Digital products may emphasize what is delivered, format, access, and usage where supplied.

Digital cards may emphasize platform, value, delivery, validity, or redemption information when supplied.

Bookings may emphasize what is being booked, who it is for, timing, and what the customer receives when supplied.

### Match Customer Intent

Describe the product in a way that supports the customer's likely decision.

Do not spend most of the description on information that has little influence on whether the customer understands or chooses the product.

---

## 10. Formatting

### Paragraphs

Group related information into readable paragraphs.

Avoid creating a paragraph for every single sentence unless the required format calls for it.

### Lists

Use lists when scanning distinct items helps the customer, such as:

- included items;
- key specifications;
- supported options;
- important features.

Do not turn naturally readable prose into lists merely for visual decoration.

### Headings

Use headings when they improve navigation in a longer description.

Do not add headings to short descriptions merely to create structure.

### Emphasis

Use emphasis sparingly and only when it improves comprehension.

Formatting should support the copy rather than compensate for weak writing.

---

## 11. Pre-QA Editorial Pass

Before submitting the description to QA:

### Remove Filler

Delete generic language that adds neither useful product information nor justified persuasion.

For example, phrases about:

> "الجودة العالية"

> "التصميم المميز"

> "التجربة الاستثنائية"

should not remain merely because they sound positive.

### Remove Redundancy

Remove repeated facts, repeated benefits, and sentences that restate an established point.

### Check Meaning

Confirm that editing for brevity has not removed an important qualification, limitation, or distinction.

### Read as a Customer

The copy should allow a customer to understand:

- what the product is;
- what matters about it;
- how relevant information connects to its use;
- any important supported limitation or distinction.

Do not add material merely because the description feels short.

A concise description can be complete.

---

## Final Boundary

`RULES.md` tells the agent **how to write**.

It does not define:

- the store's personality (`STYLE.md`);
- the approved vocabulary (`VOCABULARY.md`);
- prohibited phrases and patterns (`FORBIDDEN.md`);
- the execution workflow (`WORKFLOW.md`);
- the validation procedure (`QA.md`).

When a rule here needs a word list, prohibition list, product field, or validation test, refer to the appropriate project resource rather than duplicating it.
