# Vocabulary

## Purpose and Scope

This file defines preferred, acceptable, contextual, discouraged, and candidate vocabulary for the store's Saudi-facing product copy.

Its purpose is to help the agent choose natural and precise language when multiple Arabic expressions could communicate the same or related concept.

The target is **modern Saudi commercial Arabic**, as defined in `STYLE.md`.

This is a **contextual production lexicon**, not a dictionary of Saudi Arabic.

A term appearing in this file is **not a signal to use that term more often**. It is guidance for choosing language when the relevant meaning occurs.

The lexicon should grow from real approved store usage and deliberate editorial decisions.

---

## How to Use This File

When several expressions could communicate the same idea:

1. identify the intended meaning;
2. identify the relevant product or customer context;
3. check this file for the preferred expression or semantic distinction;
4. use the preferred term when it fits that context;
5. use an acceptable or contextual alternative when the context requires it.

Do not insert vocabulary merely because it appears here.

Do not force colloquial terms into otherwise natural sentences simply to make copy sound more Saudi.

When no relevant entry exists, use natural, clear Arabic according to `STYLE.md` and `RULES.md`. Do not invent a Saudi lexical preference merely because one is missing.

## Vocabulary Status

### Preferred

The store's first-choice term **for the specified meaning and context**.

Preferred does not mean universally correct.

### Acceptable

Natural and suitable, but not normally the first choice for the specified context.

### Contextual

Appropriate only in particular meanings, categories, tones, or situations.

### Discouraged

Understandable but consistently less suitable for the store because it may sound overly formal, translated, vague, awkward, or less natural.

Discouraged does not mean forbidden.

### Candidate

A proposed or observed term that has not yet been established as store preference.

Candidate vocabulary must not be treated as canonical.

Use this status while language is being evaluated through real copy.

### Prohibited Boundary

Expressions that must never appear belong in `FORBIDDEN.md`.

Do not turn this file into a second forbidden-expression list.

## Core Store Terminology

### Products

Use the most informative natural noun for the product.

General term:

> منتج

More specific terms may be preferable when the product type makes them clearer:

> جهاز
> قطعة
> كتاب
> دورة
> بطاقة
> اشتراك
> خدمة
> حجز

Do not use a generic noun when a more precise product type is natural and supported.

### Orders

**Preferred:** طلب

Use for a customer's purchase/order.

Examples:

> تقدر تتابع حالة طلبك من حسابك.

> تفاصيل طلبك تظهر لك بعد إتمام الشراء.

Do not use "طلب" when describing a different customer action such as downloading, booking, or activating.

### Pricing and Offers

Use vocabulary according to the actual commercial concept.

Common concepts:

> السعر
> الخصم
> العرض
> رمز الخصم
> الإجمالي

Do not substitute decorative or ambiguous wording for a precise commercial term.

### Shipping and Delivery

Distinguish:

> شحن

from:

> توصيل

**شحن** generally concerns shipping or dispatch.

**توصيل** generally concerns delivery to the customer.

Examples:

> يشحن الطلب خلال يومين.

> التوصيل متاح داخل المدينة.

Use the term that matches the actual operation.

### Availability

Distinguish:

> متوفر

from:

> متاح

**متوفر** commonly refers to physical stock or a concrete available option.

Examples:

> اللون الأسود متوفر.

> المقاس M متوفر حاليًا.

**متاح** commonly refers to access, services, appointments, options, or availability periods.

Examples:

> الحجز متاح من الأحد إلى الخميس.

> الدفع بالتقسيط متاح للطلبات المؤهلة.

These are contextual preferences, not absolute replacements.

### Customer Actions

Choose the verb that describes the real action:

> شراء
> طلب
> اختيار
> إضافة
> إتمام الطلب
> تحميل
> حجز
> استخدام
> تفعيل
> استلام

Do not use a generic action when a more precise action is available.

### Digital Delivery

Choose terminology according to what the customer actually receives:

> تحميل
> رابط تحميل
> رمز
> كود
> تفعيل
> إرسال
> استلام رقمي

Do not describe digital fulfillment using physical-shipping language unless the product genuinely has a physical component.

### Bookings and Services

Common concepts include:

> حجز
> موعد
> جلسة
> استشارة
> خدمة
> تسجيل
> حضور

Use the term that accurately describes the customer's relationship with the service.

## Saudi Commercial Expressions

### Customer-Facing Expressions

Conversational phrasing may be natural in Saudi-facing commerce.

Examples:

> إذا تبي...

> يناسبك إذا...

> تقدر...

> مناسب لـ...

These examples demonstrate possible phrasing. They are **not required Saudi markers**.

Do not deliberately insert them into descriptions where neutral wording is more natural.

### Benefit Expressions

Common constructions may include:

> يساعدك على...

> يسهل عليك...

> مناسب لـ...

> خيار عملي لـ...

Use only when the underlying claim is supported.

Do not use these constructions as generic marketing filler.

### Recommendation and Suitability

**Preferred contextual term:** مناسب

Use when the available product information supports suitability.

Examples:

> مناسب للاستخدام اليومي.

> مناسب للدوام والسفر.

Do not automatically strengthen the claim to:

> مثالي

> الأفضل

> الخيار الأمثل

unless that stronger meaning is genuinely justified and permitted.

### Convenience and Ease

Possible natural expressions include:

> سهل الاستخدام

> عملي

> بسيط

> بدون تعقيد

Use only when the product or process supports the stated quality.

### Time and Frequency

Prefer clear expressions that accurately communicate the relevant timing:

> يوميًا

> للاستخدام اليومي

> بشكل مستمر

More conversational expressions such as:

> متى ما احتجته

may be appropriate in selected contexts.

Do not use casual timing language to obscure actual delivery times, booking times, expiry, or limitations.

### Quantity and Size

Use exact product information where available.

Natural constructions may include:

> بحجم...

> بسعة...

> يضم...

> يحتوي على...

Choose according to the product and intended meaning.

## Formal vs Neutral vs Colloquial

### Neutral Arabic

Neutral written Arabic is generally preferable when:

- the product is technical;
- precision matters;
- the audience is broad;
- colloquial language would reduce clarity;
- the category requires a more serious tone.

Neutral Arabic does not mean stiff or bureaucratic Arabic.

### Saudi Colloquial

More conversational Saudi-facing phrasing may be appropriate when:

- it makes the copy more natural;
- it improves clarity or familiarity;
- the product context supports a warmer tone;
- the expression is genuinely natural in Saudi commercial writing.

Do not add colloquial language merely to demonstrate localization.

### Formal Arabic

More formal language may be appropriate for:

- medical or sensitive information;
- legal or regulatory wording;
- exact policies;
- official names;
- technical statements where precision requires it.

Formal wording should remain readable.

## English and Industry Terms

### Commonly Used Terms

Some technology, commerce, beauty, fashion, gaming, and digital-product terminology is commonly used in English or in Arabic-English hybrid form by Saudi customers.

Use such terminology when it is genuinely familiar or clearer.

### Arabic Alternatives

Prefer natural Arabic when it is clearer for the intended customer.

Do not preserve English merely to make copy appear modern.

### Preserve English When Appropriate

Keep an English term when:

- it is the recognized product or platform term;
- the translated term would be unnatural;
- customers commonly identify the concept using the English term;
- translation could alter technical meaning.

Specific recurring terminology should be recorded as individual entries in this file.

## Semantic Distinctions

When two words are commonly treated as interchangeable but imply different meanings, record the distinction.

### مناسب / مثالي

**مناسب:** suitable.

**مثالي:** ideal; stronger.

Default to "مناسب" unless the stronger claim is genuinely justified.

### متوفر / متاح

**متوفر:** commonly used for stock, physical availability, or a concrete option.

**متاح:** commonly used for access, services, appointments, options, or periods.

Choose according to meaning.

### شحن / توصيل

**شحن:** shipping/dispatch.

**توصيل:** delivery to the customer.

Use the term matching the actual operation.

### شراء / طلب

**شراء:** purchasing.

**طلب:** placing an order.

Choose according to the actual customer action.

### استخدام / استلام

**استخدام:** using the product or service.

**استلام:** receiving or collecting it.

Do not use one term to imply the other.

**Meaning:** The concept expressed by the term.

**Use when:** Relevant meaning and context.

**Avoid when:** Contexts where another term is more precise or natural.

**Alternatives:** Other suitable terms, when relevant.

**Examples:**

- Example 1
- Example 2

**Notes:** Important semantic, category, or tonal distinctions.

An entry should exist because it helps make a real lexical decision.

## Maintenance Rules

### Evidence First

Prefer vocabulary decisions grounded in:

- approved store copy;
- repeated editorial choices;
- established Saudi commercial usage;
- recurring customer-facing terminology.

Do not establish a store preference merely because a model claims that a word is "Saudi."

### Distinguish Observation From Canon

An expression may be observed in Saudi usage without becoming a preferred store expression.

Use `Candidate` status until it has been deliberately evaluated.

### Record Context

When a term is preferred only in a particular meaning, category, or tone, record that context.

Avoid global replacement rules unless a preference is genuinely store-wide.

### Prefer Semantic Precision

When two natural terms communicate meaningfully different concepts, document the distinction.

### Avoid Redundancy

Do not create separate entries for trivial grammatical variants when one contextual entry can cover the decision.

### Keep Status Meaningful

Do not mark a term "discouraged" merely because another term is preferred.

Reserve discouraged status for choices that are consistently less suitable.

### Separate Vocabulary From Prohibition

Move expressions that must never appear into `FORBIDDEN.md`.

This file focuses on lexical choice, not enforcement.

### Learn From the Golden Set

Repeated successful wording in approved golden-set examples may justify a vocabulary entry.

One example does not automatically establish a universal rule.

### Never Inflate Usage

The presence of a term in this file must never be interpreted as a recommendation to use it more frequently.

The lexicon tells the agent **what to choose when the relevant concept occurs**.

## Starter Lexicon

These are initial working entries. They are deliberately small and should be refined through real store work.

### مناسب

**Status:** Preferred

**Meaning:** Suitable or appropriate for a stated use or context.

**Use when:** Product information supports the stated suitability.

**Avoid when:** The intended meaning would be a guarantee or universal suitability.

**Examples:**

- مناسب للاستخدام اليومي.
- مناسب للدوام والسفر.

**Note:** Do not automatically strengthen this to "مثالي" or "الأفضل".

### مثالي

**Status:** Contextual

**Meaning:** Ideal; stronger than "مناسب".

**Use when:** The stronger claim is genuinely justified and permitted.

**Avoid when:** "مناسب" communicates the intended meaning.

### متوفر

**Status:** Contextual

**Meaning:** Present or concretely available.

**Use when:** Referring to stock, size, color, or another concrete option.

**Examples:**

- اللون الأسود متوفر.
- المقاس M متوفر حاليًا.

### متاح

**Status:** Contextual

**Meaning:** Available or accessible.

**Use when:** Referring to services, access, booking times, options, or availability periods.

**Examples:**

- الحجز متاح من الأحد إلى الخميس.
- الدفع بالتقسيط متاح للطلبات المؤهلة.

### شحن

**Status:** Contextual

**Meaning:** Shipping or dispatch.

**Use when:** The context concerns movement/dispatch of an order.

### توصيل

**Status:** Contextual

**Meaning:** Delivery.

**Use when:** The context concerns delivery to the customer.

### طلب

**Status:** Preferred

**Meaning:** Customer order.

**Examples:**

- تابع حالة طلبك.
- تقدر تشوف تفاصيل طلبك من حسابك.

### حجز

**Status:** Preferred

**Meaning:** Booking or reservation.

**Use when:** The customer reserves a service, appointment, course, seat, or other bookable item.

### تحميل

**Status:** Contextual

**Meaning:** Downloading digital content.

**Use when:** The customer receives a digital file or product through download.

### تفعيل

**Status:** Contextual

**Meaning:** Activation.

**Use when:** A code, card, account feature, or other product requires activation.

### يسهل عليك

**Status:** Contextual

**Meaning:** Makes something easier for the customer.

**Use when:** The product feature directly supports the stated convenience.

**Avoid when:** Convenience is only an assumed benefit.

### عملي

**Status:** Contextual

**Meaning:** Practical.

**Use when:** Product characteristics support the description for a stated use.

**Avoid when:** Used as empty praise.

### بدون تعقيد

**Status:** Contextual

**Meaning:** Without unnecessary complexity.

**Use when:** The product or process genuinely has a simple experience.

**Avoid when:** Used as generic marketing filler.

### إذا تبي

**Status:** Candidate

**Meaning:** Conversational "if you want".

**Use when:** The surrounding sentence genuinely benefits from this Saudi-facing conversational construction.

**Avoid when:** Added mechanically to make copy sound Saudi.

### تقدر

**Status:** Candidate

**Meaning:** "You can / you are able to."

**Use when:** Directly describing a real customer capability supported by the product or service.

**Avoid when:** The capability depends on unstated conditions.

### مرّة

**Status:** Candidate

**Meaning:** Conversational intensifier meaning "very".

**Use when:** It is naturally appropriate to the surrounding sentence and tone.

**Avoid when:** Inserted merely to increase perceived Saudi-ness.
