# Forbidden

## Purpose and Scope

This file defines output patterns that must not appear in Saudi-facing product copy.

It establishes the hard negative boundary of the writing system.

A forbidden pattern is output that is misleading, unsupported, manipulative, artificially localized, materially unnatural, or otherwise incompatible with the project's standards.

The primary unit of this file is the **underlying pattern**, not the exact phrase.

An example phrase is illustrative. Replacing it with a synonym that performs the same prohibited behavior does not make the output acceptable.

`STYLE.md` defines the desired voice.

`RULES.md` defines how to write.

`VOCABULARY.md` defines preferred lexical choices.

`WORKFLOW.md` defines the execution process.

`QA.md` defines how violations are detected and evaluated.

This file defines what must not be produced.

---

## How Forbidden Rules Work

### Patterns, Not Blacklists

A word is not automatically forbidden because it sometimes appears in bad copy.

For example:

> "مميز"

may be valid when the product information supports a meaningful distinguishing characteristic.

The forbidden pattern is vague positive language that makes an unsupported or meaningless product claim.

### Unsupported vs Inherently Invalid

Most promotional language is not inherently invalid.

A claim becomes forbidden when it is:

* unsupported;
* misleading;
* materially exaggerated;
* presented with unjustified certainty;
* inappropriate to the available evidence or product context.

Some behaviors are prohibited regardless of wording, such as inventing product facts or fabricating reviews.

### Forbidden Means Do Not Finalize

When a forbidden pattern appears:

1. remove or rewrite the offending content;
2. preserve factual meaning;
3. run the applicable QA checks again.

### Do Not Circumvent

Do not replace a forbidden phrase with a synonym that performs the same prohibited behavior.

The restriction follows the underlying claim or behavior.

## 1. Fabrication and Unsupported Facts

### Invented Specifications

Never invent:

* sizes;
* dimensions;
* weights;
* capacities;
* materials;
* ingredients;
* compatibility;
* features;
* included items;
* technical capabilities.

Do not infer a product specification merely because it is common for similar products.

### Invented Benefits

Do not convert a product characteristic into a stronger benefit that the source does not establish.

Examples:

> "مصنوع من مادة X"
> → "مريح جدًا"

> "بطارية 5000mAh"
> → "يضمن لك استخدامًا طوال اليوم"

> "مقاوم للماء"
> → "يحمي جهازك مهما كانت الظروف"

The characteristic may be stated when supported.

The unsupported outcome may not.

### Invented Compatibility

Do not claim compatibility with devices, systems, platforms, sizes, environments, or services unless supported.

Do not turn category membership into universal compatibility.

### Invented Outcomes

Do not promise outcomes that the available product information does not establish.

This includes unsupported claims about:

* performance;
* satisfaction;
* health results;
* financial results;
* durability;
* effectiveness;
* improvement.

---

## 2. Misleading Certainty

### Guarantees

Do not use absolute guarantees unless the supplied information and task explicitly support them.

Examples of risky patterns:

> مضمون 100%

> يضمن لك...

> أكيد راح...

> مستحيل...

The exact wording may change; the prohibition concerns unjustified certainty.

### Universal Claims

Do not imply universal suitability or universal effectiveness without evidence.

Examples:

> مناسب للجميع

> يناسب كل الاحتياجات

> الخيار المثالي لأي شخص

### Unqualified Safety

Do not make unsupported absolute safety claims.

Examples:

> آمن تمامًا

> بدون أي آثار جانبية

> خالٍ من المخاطر

A legitimate safety-related fact may be stated when explicitly supported. The forbidden behavior is turning partial information into absolute safety.

### Unqualified Performance

Do not make unsupported absolute performance claims such as:

> يعمل دائمًا

> لن يتعطل

> يستمر بلا توقف

unless the information genuinely establishes that level of certainty.

---

## 3. Unsupported Promotional Claims

### Unsupported Superlatives

Do not make unsupported claims such as:

> الأفضل

> الأقوى

> الأجود

> الأحدث

> رقم 1

> لا مثيل له

> الأول من نوعه

These expressions are not universally forbidden words. They are forbidden when the claim is not explicitly supported and appropriate.

### Empty Positive Claims

Do not use positive adjectives as a substitute for product information.

Examples of the pattern:

> جودة عالية

> تصميم رائع

> أداء مميز

> تجربة استثنائية

> منتج فاخر

when the statement is unsupported, vague, or disconnected from a specific product characteristic.

A specific, supported characterization is allowed.

### Fabricated Authority

Never invent:

* awards;
* certifications;
* expert endorsements;
* official recommendations;
* test results;
* rankings;
* professional approval.

### Fabricated Social Proof

Never invent:

* customer counts;
* review counts;
* star ratings;
* popularity statistics;
* "most purchased" claims;
* testimonials;
* customer quotes.

---

## 4. Manipulative Commerce

### Fake Urgency

Do not invent urgency.

Examples:

> آخر فرصة!

> باقي ساعات فقط!

> لا تفوتها الآن!

> اطلب قبل ما تروح عليك!

These may only appear when the underlying promotion or availability information genuinely establishes the relevant condition and the wording is appropriate.

### Fake Scarcity

Do not invent:

> الكمية محدودة

> آخر حبة

> باقي عدد قليل

when stock or availability information does not support scarcity.

### Pressure

Do not use unsupported pressure tactics such as:

> لازم تشتريه

> ما راح تندم

> إذا ما أخذته أنت الخسران

> مستحيل تلقى أفضل منه

### Fear-Based Selling

Do not create fear solely to increase conversion.

This includes unsupported fear about:

* losing money;
* missing opportunities;
* health consequences;
* safety;
* social status;
* being left behind.

---

## 5. Generic AI Marketing Slop

### Empty Praise

Do not use broad positive language that adds no product-specific value.

The issue is not the adjective itself. The issue is using generic praise instead of useful information.

### Generic Introductions

Do not begin with text that could be pasted onto almost any product.

Examples:

> اكتشف تجربة جديدة...

> إذا كنت تبحث عن الجودة والتميز...

> نقدم لك الحل المثالي...

The copy should establish the actual product or a supported reason to care.

### Generic Conclusions

Do not add a generic ending simply because the description feels incomplete.

Examples:

> لا تفوت الفرصة!

> اطلبه الآن واستمتع بالتجربة!

> خيارك الأمثل يبدأ من هنا!

Use a closing only when it contributes legitimate product or task value.

### Formulaic Benefit Stacking

Do not automatically produce sequences such as:

> جودة + تصميم + أداء + راحة + تجربة استثنائية

without product-specific evidence.

### Repetitive Claims

Do not repeat the same benefit through multiple near-synonymous sentences to increase length or perceived richness.

---

## 6. Artificial Localization

### Forced Saudi Slang

Do not insert Saudi colloquial expressions merely to make a description appear more local.

A conversational term is not forbidden merely because it is colloquial.

It becomes forbidden when the expression is artificially inserted, awkward in context, or used mainly to signal "Saudi."

### Slang Density

Do not deliberately increase dialect or slang density to demonstrate Saudi identity.

The target is natural Saudi-facing commerce, not maximum dialect.

### Stereotyped Saudi Voice

Do not treat Saudi commercial language as a caricature of:

* exaggerated friendliness;
* exaggerated masculinity;
* excessive humor;
* performative local identity;
* slang-heavy speech.

### Fake Familiarity

Do not create an artificial personal relationship with the customer through forced intimacy, teasing, or exaggerated friendliness.

---

## 7. Translation and Language Failure

### Literal Translation

Do not preserve source-language syntax when it produces unnatural Arabic.

Translate the meaning, not the sentence structure.

### Inflated Corporate Arabic

Do not turn simple product information into bureaucratic or corporate prose merely to sound professional.

For example, avoid constructions such as:

> "يُسهم هذا المنتج في تلبية الاحتياجات المتنوعة..."

when simpler natural wording expresses the same meaning.

### Unnatural Hybrids

Do not combine Arabic and English mechanically in ways that sound translated or artificial.

### Decorative English

Do not insert English terminology merely to sound modern, premium, or technologically sophisticated.

Use English terminology when it is genuinely natural or technically appropriate.

---

## 8. Inappropriate or High-Risk Claims

### Medical and Health

Do not make unsupported claims about:

* treatment;
* cure;
* prevention;
* diagnosis;
* therapeutic effects;
* health outcomes;
* medical effectiveness.

Legitimate medical terminology describing the product or service is not itself forbidden.

The prohibition concerns unsupported medical claims.

### Financial

Do not make unsupported claims about:

* guaranteed savings;
* guaranteed profit;
* investment outcomes;
* guaranteed financial results.

### Safety

Do not claim complete or absolute safety without explicit support.

### Legal and Regulatory

Do not invent:

* approvals;
* certifications;
* licenses;
* regulatory status;
* government authorization;
* legal compliance.

### Guaranteed Results

Do not promise results that depend on:

* user behavior;
* external circumstances;
* professional judgment;
* setup;
* future events;
* conditions not established by the product source.

---

## 9. Irrelevant or Misleading Comparisons

### Competitors

Do not introduce competitor names or comparisons unless explicitly required by the task and supported by the source.

Do not claim superiority without evidence.

### Market Claims

Do not claim:

> الأكثر مبيعًا

> الأكثر شعبية

> رقم 1 في السعودية

> الأشهر في السوق

without appropriate supporting evidence.

### Category Generalizations

Do not make unsupported negative or positive claims about an entire product category to make the current product appear better.

Examples:

> "معظم المنتجات الأخرى سيئة."

> "ما فيه منتج في السوق يعطيك هذا."

Such claims require evidence and are generally unnecessary.

---

## 10. Source and Product Boundary Violations

### Cross-Product Leakage

Never transfer product facts between products because they appear similar.

Specifications, ingredients, capacities, terms, or service conditions belong to their own product source.

### Golden-Set Fact Leakage

Golden-set examples demonstrate approved writing quality.

Their product facts must never be copied into a new product unless independently supported by that product's source.

### Invention From Category Knowledge

General knowledge about a product class is not evidence that a specific product possesses a particular characteristic.

### Source Mutation

Do not alter, normalize, invent, or "repair" source product information merely to make copy generation easier.

If the source appears incorrect or incomplete, surface the issue.

## 11. Explicit Examples

The examples below illustrate prohibited **patterns**. Equivalent patterns expressed with different words remain prohibited.

### Unsupported Superlative

**Forbidden**

> "اكتشف أفضل تجربة على الإطلاق مع هذا المنتج الرائع الذي يجمع بين الجودة والفخامة والأداء."

Problems:

* unsupported superlative;
* generic praise;
* vague benefits;
* product-independent wording.

**Acceptable direction**

> "عطر [اسم المنتج] يأتي بتركيبة تحتوي على [المعلومات المدعومة]، ومناسب للاستخدام المسائي."

The difference is evidence and specificity, not simply weaker adjectives.

### Unsupported Guarantee

**Forbidden**

> "إذا تبي جهاز مضمون 100% ويشتغل مع كل شيء، هذا هو خيارك."

**Acceptable direction**

> "يدعم [الأجهزة/الأنظمة المدعومة]، وتقدر تستخدمه لـ[الاستخدام المدعوم]."

### Fake Scarcity

**Forbidden**

> "باقي آخر حبتين، اطلبه الآن قبل ما يخلص!"

when no stock information establishes scarcity.

**Acceptable direction**

Omit scarcity unless it is supported.

### Forced Localization

**Forbidden**

> "يا بعدي هذا رهيب مرّة وبيخليك ما تستغني عنه."

when the wording is being used merely to perform Saudi-ness or exaggerated familiarity.

**Acceptable direction**

Use natural Saudi-facing commercial language appropriate to the product without deliberately maximizing slang.

### Medical Overclaim

**Forbidden**

> "هذا الكريم يعالج المشكلة نهائيًا ويضمن لك نتائج مضمونة."

when the product information does not establish those medical outcomes.

**Acceptable direction**

State the supported product characteristics or intended use without promising an unsupported medical result.

## 12. Maintenance Rules

### Maintain Patterns, Not an Endless Blacklist

Add new entries when a recurring failure represents a meaningful pattern.

Do not add every bad sentence individually.

### Keep Context

When a phrase is forbidden only under certain circumstances, document the circumstance.

### Avoid False Positives

Do not forbid a legitimate expression merely because it can appear in bad copy.

The prohibition should target the underlying behavior.

### Update From Real Failures

New forbidden patterns should preferably arise from:

* observed model failures;
* rejected store copy;
* repeated QA failures;
* deliberate editorial decisions.

### Do Not Duplicate QA

Do not add regexes, scoring thresholds, test procedures, or implementation details here.

Those belong in `QA.md`.

### Do Not Duplicate Vocabulary

If a term is merely discouraged or contextually preferred, record it in `VOCABULARY.md`.

Use this file when the underlying output pattern crosses a hard quality, truthfulness, or integrity boundary.

### Re-evaluate the Rule Set

When a new prohibition is proposed, ask:

1. Is this actually a hard boundary?
2. Is the underlying behavior clear?
3. Could a legitimate use produce a false positive?
4. Does the rule belong here rather than in vocabulary or QA?
5. Does it protect against a recurring or meaningful failure?
